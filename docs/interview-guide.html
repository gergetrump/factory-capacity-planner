01 · Start with the decision

What should we make, where, and when?

A planner has orders due at the end of each shift. Several manufacturing cells can make some products, at different speeds. Machine time and operators are limited. A known outage may remove a cell tomorrow.

The tool assigns whole product units to eligible cells and shifts. It can make stock early, carry unmet demand forward, and compare an original scenario with an outage. The chosen objective is minimize accumulated backlog.

Known demand & resources→Production decisions→Independent checks→Dashboard & saved plan

What you can do in the app

  • Choose a bundled synthetic example.
  • Remove one cell’s capacity over a shift window.
  • Run Gurobi, then the original greedy method, on the same captured input.
  • Inspect allocations, stock/backlog, machine minutes, staffing and solver status.
  • Retain original/outage results and export the exact solved snapshot.

What the app does not do

  • No named employees or exact start/end times.
  • No real factory data, SQL database or operational ingestion pipeline.
  • No material shortages, storage limits, setups or yield losses.
  • No optimized energy, monetary cost or workforce savings.
  • No hosted solver or production deployment.

One product, one eligible cell, two shifts, no initial stock. One unit takes one minute. An active cell needs one operator for the whole shift. Demand is newly due at each shift end. Total production cannot exceed total demand.

The lab selects a least-inventory plan among tied minimum-backlog allocations only to make its display stable. The real Gurobi app has no inventory tie-breaker and may choose another equally good plan.

How the arithmetic works

net stock = previous net stock + production − new demand
inventory = max(net stock, 0)
backlog = max(−net stock, 0)
objective = backlog after T1 + backlog after T2

A piece overdue at two shift boundaries counts twice. Therefore accumulated backlog has units unit-shifts. Final backlog counts only units still overdue after the last shift. Neither number is automatically a monetary cost or hours late.

Worked answer: canonical lookahead case

T1 has one minute and no due demand. T2 needs one unit but has zero capacity. Make one unit in T1, hold it, then supply T2. Inventory is [1,0], backlog [0,0], objective 0. Since backlog is nonnegative, no plan can score below 0: this construction proves the optimum by hand.

The due-only greedy method makes nothing in T1 because nothing is due. It cannot produce in T2 and ends with backlog 1. If you add the no-inventory rule, the zero-backlog plan is removed and the restricted optimum becomes 1. Backlog keeps the restricted model feasible.

03 · Turn a physical rule into a constraint

The full mathematical model

Use products p, cells c and shifts t as indices. Processing time a[p,c] exists only for eligible pairs. Inputs are fixed facts; decision variables are values the optimizer chooses.

InputMeaning and unit
a[p,c]Integer minutes per unit on an eligible cell.
C[c,t]Available machine minutes, no greater than the shift duration.
r[c], W[t]Fixed crew required by a cell; available operator headcount.
d[p,t], I0[p]Newly due units; initial inventory units. Initial backlog is zero.
DecisionDomainPhysical interpretation
x[p,c,t]Nonnegative integerWhole units made on an eligible cell in a shift.
y[c,t]Binary 0/1Cell is active and reserves its full crew.
I[p,t], B[p,t]Nonnegative integersEnd-of-shift inventory and backlog.
z[p,t]Binary 0/1Selects whether inventory or backlog may be positive.

Tight, explainable bounds

Q[p] = max(0, Σₜ d[p,t] − I0[p])
U[p,c,t] = min(Q[p], floor(C[c,t] / a[p,c]))
Icap[p] = I0[p] + Q[p]
Bcap[p,t] = cumulative demand through t

Q limits useful total production. If initial stock is 3 and total horizon demand is 8, making more than 5 adds unused stock. This reasoning depends on no initial backlog, terminal stock target or beyond-horizon demand. Add one of those requirements and revisit the bound.

Machine capacity and activation
Σₚ a[p,c] × x[p,c,t] ≤ C[c,t] × y[c,t]
x[p,c,t] ≤ U[p,c,t] × y[c,t]
y[c,t] ≤ Σₚ x[p,c,t]

All products share a cell’s minutes. If y=0, production must be zero. The last rule requires an active cell to produce at least one whole unit, so idle activations do not inflate activity metrics. The pairwise activation rule also links each decision using a tight upper bound rather than an unexplained huge constant.

Example: 25 P1 at 8 minutes plus 15 P3 at 9 minutes uses 25×8 + 15×9 = 335 minutes on C2, within its 420-minute capacity.

Staffing is reserved once per cell
Σ꜀ r[c] × y[c,t] ≤ W[t]

A cell making two products still reserves its crew once. C2 needs 2 operators and C3 needs 4, so running both reserves 6 of 7 available. Using half a shift’s machine minutes does not reserve half a crew. The model does not schedule breaks or identify individual workers.

Balance, carryover and exclusivity
I[p,t] − B[p,t] = previous net stock + Σ꜀ x[p,c,t] − d[p,t]
I[p,t] ≤ Icap[p] × z[p,t]
B[p,t] ≤ Bcap[p,t] × (1 − z[p,t])

Previous net stock is I0 in the first shift, then previous I−B. If a shift starts with net −3, makes 5 and gets 4 new due units, it ends at −2: inventory 0, backlog 2.

The sign selector prevents reporting positive inventory and positive backlog for the same product at the same time. Minimizing backlog discourages that at an optimum, but does not exclude it from every unfinished feasible incumbent. At I=B=0, either value of z may fit; it is a modeling device, not a business KPI.

Useful production and the single objective
Σ꜀,ₜ x[p,c,t] ≤ Q[p]
minimize Σₚ,ₜ B[p,t]

The objective counts each overdue unit at each shift boundary. It weights products equally and ignores backlog after the finite horizon. Storage and cell activity are not minimized. Two plans with backlog 0 may use different stock or active cells and still be equally optimal.

There is no automatic preference for less stock, fewer staff, less energy or lower cost. A new preference requires an explicit business policy, formulation and validation—not a claim based on incidental output differences.

Why MILP?

All expressions are linear: input constants multiply variables; variables are not multiplied together. Whole units and binary activation make it a mixed-integer linear program. A linear program with integrality relaxed could use fractional production or activation and give a lower bound, but such a solution might reserve a physically impossible fraction of a fixed crew.

Check your understanding: no capacity anywhere

Correct: backlog is allowed. Zero production, inactive cells, and accumulated net-stock states form a feasible plan. An infeasible or unbounded solver termination therefore signals a model/execution problem under the current assumptions.

04 · Explain the operational decision

A small factory, three different outcomes

The app’s base example has four products, three cells and ten shifts. Every cell has 420 machine minutes within each 480-minute shift. There are seven available operators per shift, no initial stock and constant new demand.

Actual bundled base input
ProductDemand each shiftEligible minutes per unit
P125 unitsC1: 6; C2: 8
P220 unitsC1: 10; C3: 7
P315 unitsC2: 9; C3: 12
P410 unitsC3: 15 only

Crew requirements are C1: 3, C2: 2, C3: 4. Having enough total machine minutes is insufficient if the useful cell combination requires too many operators.

Base: a hand construction proves zero backlog is possible

Every shift, C2 makes P1 and P3: 25×8 + 15×9 = 335 minutes. C3 makes P2 and P4: 20×7 + 10×15 = 290 minutes. Their combined crew is 2+4=6 ≤ 7. All demand is met each shift, so backlog 0 is feasible and optimal.

These are hand-explainable allocations; Gurobi may return a different tied zero-backlog plan.

Why can the original greedy lose even without an outage?

Its first shift makes P4 on its only cell C3, selects fast C1 for P1, then makes P2 and part of P3 on already-active C3. C1 and C3 reserve all seven operators, so C2 cannot start.

The saved first-shift greedy output is C1/P1=25, C3/P2=20, C3/P3=10 and C3/P4=10. C3 uses 410 minutes and P3 ends five units late. The local cell choices missed the globally useful C2+C3 combination. The full-horizon greedy objective is 51 unit-shifts, with 3 units finally overdue.

This is a specific weakness of this deterministic heuristic, not proof that all heuristics fail.

Short outage: absorb a known interruption with inventory

C3 is unavailable in T5–T6. P4 has no alternative cell. Build its needed 20 units in advance: make 15 P4 rather than 10 in each of T1–T4. Keep other production as in the hand construction.

This slider checks the proposed construction, not an optimization run. More than five extra units per early shift can also cover the outage, but uses additional early inventory and displaces some later P4 production. The displayed C3 early-load rule must still hold.

During T5–T6, C1 can make P1/P2 in 25×6 + 20×10 = 350 minutes. C2 makes P3 in 15×9 = 135 minutes. Their crew is 5. The prepared P4 stock covers demand when enough was made early. After T6, normal product-to-cell routes resume. If the slider adds more than five P4 per early shift, reduce later P4 production by the excess inventory so total P4 production remains its 100-unit horizon demand. For example, six extra per early shift leaves four units after T6; make six rather than ten P4 in T7.

Full outage: derive the unavoidable lower bound

If C3 is unavailable for all ten shifts, P4 cannot be made anywhere and has no initial stock. Its backlog is 10,20,…,100 units. The sum is 10×(1+…+10)=550 unit-shifts, with 100 units finally overdue.

Other products fit on C1/P1/P2 and C2/P3 with crew 5, so 550 is both a lower bound and achievable objective. All three benchmark methods attain it. This is a sanity check, not an optimization improvement.

06 · Separate physical validity from proof quality

When can you trust a result?

An optimizer returns variable values and termination information. The app checks both. A beautiful dashboard alone cannot tell you whether a plan is physically feasible or proven best.

  1. Extract actual values. Production, activation and stock/backlog come from solver variables. Reject nonfinite values or values more than 1e−5 from an integer, then round within that tolerance.
  2. Check physical arithmetic. Verify quantities, keys, eligibility, shared minutes, crew, activation, production limits, stock states and metrics using integer arithmetic.
  3. Interpret termination. Compare the checked objective with the raw objective and lower bound. Only accept optimal under the required status and zero-gap settings.

Incumbent, bound and gap

In a minimization problem, a feasible incumbent gives an upper bound on the optimum. The solver’s lower bound rules out objectives below a threshold. The true optimum lies between them. A bound is not itself a production plan.

For a positive incumbent J and nonnegative bound L, the displayed relative gap is (J−L)/J. When both are zero, use the solver’s zero-gap result rather than dividing by zero. Numerical conventions and tolerances still matter.

StatusInterpretation
optimalChecked feasible incumbent; Gurobi optimal termination, zero configured relative/absolute gaps and matching objective/bound.
feasibleAccepted checked incumbent, optimum unproven under that termination.
heuristic_feasibleChecked heuristic plan; no solver proof metadata.
no_solutionNo accepted incumbent. Do not invent a plan from a bound.
solver_unavailableImport, initialization or license unavailable; no automatic substitution.
solver_errorExecution/termination or objective/bound inconsistency.
invalid_resultInvalid extracted decisions or failed independent validation.
How independent is the checker?

It does not import Gurobi or reuse model-building expressions. It directly checks physical constraints. However, make_solution is also used to build reports and to reconstruct expected stock/metrics inside the checker. A shared reporting bug could agree with itself.

Small exhaustive oracles, corrupted-plan tests, hand constructions and a later direct raw-JSON audit complement that checker. They do not independently certify every large model’s optimum. planner check verifies feasibility and arithmetic, not authenticity of edited solver metadata.

Which claim does “13 with lower bound 0” justify?

Correct: 0 is a lower bound, not a demonstrated attainable objective. A 100% relative gap can coexist with only 13 unit-shifts of absolute uncertainty. The result remains unproven optimal.

07 · Discuss what the experiment actually establishes

The benchmarks, without overstating them

13 fixed synthetic inputs × 3 methods × 3 measured repeats = 117 runs. All 117 saved plans passed physical feasibility checks. Of the 39 Gurobi runs, 33 were proven optimal and six stopped at the two-second optimization limit with checked incumbents.

Original greedy

Deterministic, due-demand only. Orders products by available eligibility/shortage and favors already-active cells, then processing speed. It cannot prepare stock for demand that is not yet due.

Stronger dispatch portfolio

Includes original greedy plus urgency/scarcity variants at windows 1, 2 and the full horizon. It enumerates crew-feasible cell subsets and uses a projected-backlog proxy assuming no more production in that window.

The portfolio checks all completed plans and selects the lowest actual full-horizon backlog. It therefore cannot do worse than included greedy. That is a construction property, not a discovered universal advantage. It is not an online policy, an industrial baseline or an optimum oracle. It appears only in benchmark scripts, not as a third dashboard method.

What makes the procedure useful?

  • Exact fixtures and full plans are saved, with source/input hashes and environment versions.
  • One tiny warmup per method is excluded. Methods rotate order so each runs first, second and third once per case.
  • Total planning wall time includes model/environment setup, extraction and checking. Gurobi’s internal Runtime measures optimize separately.
  • Output serialization, fixture/output I/O and a second saved-output audit are outside timing.
  • Failures, timeouts, bounds, ties and the slow portfolio case are retained.

What should an interviewer challenge?

  • There are 13 distinct inputs, not 117 independent problems. The eight generated inputs use only one seed per profile.
  • Only three timing repeats on one shared host; no CPU isolation or confidence intervals.
  • Scales change demand, rates and eligibility density. The largest cases are deliberately dense, not a controlled scaling curve.
  • Gurobi has a two-second optimization budget; portfolio has a fixed candidate set without a matching time cap. This is not an equal-budget algorithm contest.
  • Balanced cases mostly tie at zero, so show size/feasibility coverage rather than broad superiority.
  • Maximum tested size is the app’s ceiling: 8 products, 6 cells, 20 shifts; 1,560 variables and 1,708 constraints before presolve. No industrial-scale claim follows.

A defensible interview sentence: “We compared checked plans on 13 fixed synthetic scenarios and recorded quality/runtime tradeoffs. On the largest disrupted case, Gurobi found backlog 13 versus the portfolio’s 573, but its bound was 0, so that incumbent was not proven optimal.”

Read the full benchmark report and raw measurements. Do not pool unrelated scenario totals into a factory improvement percentage.

08 · Preserve the relationship between input and evidence

Dashboard state and failure behavior

The important UI behavior is keeping each plan paired with the exact scenario that produced it. React maintains an editable draft, captured original/outage comparisons, the inspected scenario and the selected method.

  1. Choose a fixture. The app copies it into the draft and initializes outage controls.
  2. Apply outage. Capacity rows in one chosen cell/window become zero, starting from the bundled original. Merely changing a dropdown is not the same as applying the edit.
  3. Run comparison. Copy the draft once; send Gurobi and then greedy that same snapshot. Each outcome is retained separately.
  4. Edit after running. A changed applied input makes the saved result stale. It remains a valid record of its original scenario.
  5. Export. Download the saved returned result, including its input, rather than the currently editable controls.

This is an explanatory state walkthrough, not a live API or the production React app.

Failure is a result state, not an excuse to change the evidence

EventActual behavior / boundary
Invalid requestPydantic rejects it; HTTP 422. Missing rows or bad references are not silently repaired.

Wrong method/input in responseThe client rejects the response as incomparable. Object-key order is normalized; array order is retained.

Why keep the API synchronous?

It is a small local tool with bounded inputs and one admitted solve per process. A queue, subprocess supervision, cancellation or restart recovery would create more obligations. Those could become requirements for a real multiuser service, but are not implemented here. Local simplicity is reasonable if its limits are clear.

You change T5–T6 to T5–T10 and apply the edit, but do not solve. What should the old download contain?

Correct: export is an evidence snapshot. Replacing only its input with a newer edit would make the saved plan misleading. Mark it stale relative to the draft and retain its solved input.

Algorithms named in the job posting

LP, MILP, simplex and branch-and-bound

An LP uses linear constraints/objective and continuous variables. Simplex is one way to solve LPs; barrier methods are another. An MILP adds integer restrictions. A solver may solve relaxations for lower bounds, branch on integer decisions and prune subproblems whose bounds cannot beat the incumbent; modern methods add cuts and heuristics.

Gurobi supplies these optimization algorithms. This project implements a formulation and its software integration, not a new simplex or branch-and-bound solver. Do not claim which internal algorithm solved a particular run unless you have inspected the relevant configuration/log.

CP-SAT, Hungarian and shortest path

CP-SAT combines constraint-programming ideas, SAT-based reasoning and integer optimization techniques, and can be useful for logical/discrete scheduling constraints. It is not implemented here; this repository does not establish proficiency with it.

The Hungarian algorithm solves a classical minimum-cost assignment problem. Shortest-path algorithms find low-cost routes in a graph under their respective assumptions. Both are useful OR tools, but the current model’s multi-shift shared capacities, crews and inventory require more structure than a simple one-to-one assignment or path problem.

Be ready to explain these concepts at your actual level. “I know the basic idea and would need to learn the API” is better than treating keywords as implemented experience.
